Production coding model evaluation

Keep Terra as the default. Make quality escalation explicit.

The expanded production cohort found real role-specific challengers, but no cheaper route matched the incumbent quality range consistently enough to replace Terra across domains. Sol remains the clearest premium route when repair risk dominates model spend.

What to do

Optimize the default for the common case, then preserve obvious escape hatches for work where a failed first pass is expensive.

01

Pilot Terra xhigh

Use it as the general implementation route. Keep the selection visible and overridable.

02

Expose Sol high

Use it for correctness-sensitive, weakly testable, or repair-expensive changes.

03

Keep advisors off

Independent review stays explicit. The retained causal study did not justify automatic advisor cost.

04

Inherit the controller

Planning, specialists, compaction, and long-running work should not get speculative model defaults.

05

Measure downstream cost

Track repair time, retries, time-to-valid-patch, review, and time-to-merge—not tokens alone.

06

Confirm role boundaries

Run matched follow-ups only where a challenger already landed in the champion's range.

The retention rule: a challenger cannot be cut from a role when its mean is within five points of the champion or its per-trial judge distribution overlaps the champion's, provided deterministic and source-reconciled evidence agrees. Cost and speed rank same-range routes; they do not eliminate them.

What this does not prove

The evaluation is production-shaped, not the full shipping loop. The recommendation is deliberately narrower than the dataset.

No shipping-loop parity

We reconstructed merged changes and validated patches; we did not take every route through CI, review, repair, and release.

No planner winner

The current matched planning refresh is incomplete. Planning should inherit the controller for now.

No provider transfer

A model and serving path are one treatment. Baseten evidence does not automatically transfer to Fireworks.

How quality and value were judged
Every production artifact received deterministic validation, a three-model blind panel (Fable 5 xhigh, Sol high, Opus 5 high), and source reconciliation. Deterministic evidence and actual defect class outrank the score mean. Quality:Value and weighted quality/cost/speed are exact-cohort relative; higher is better and rank 1 is best.
Why Terra wins despite Sol's higher quality
The repeated switch rule required every threshold to pass. Sol met completion, quality, time/stability, and developer-control thresholds, but its cost per valid result was 1.349× Terra—above the locked 1.25× ceiling. Terra's selection is therefore a policy decision under candidate spend, not proof that its output quality equals Sol's.
Privacy-safe evidence

Public artifacts contain aggregate outcomes only. Prompts, patches, repository identifiers, and source adjudications remain private.
